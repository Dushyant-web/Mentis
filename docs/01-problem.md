# 01 · The problem

## The short version

Dyslexia and dysgraphia are common, they are invisible, and in India almost nobody gets
checked. Not because the law is missing — the law exists — but because the *instrument*
is missing.

## The numbers

| Figure | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Children in school in India | **24.69 crore** across 14.71 lakh schools | UDISE+ 2024–25, Ministry of Education |
| Pooled prevalence of specific learning disability | **8%** | Scaria, Bhaskaran & George — systematic review and meta-analysis, *Indian J Psychological Medicine*, 2023 |
| → Children who may need assessing | **≈ 2 crore** | arithmetic on the two rows above |
| RCI-registered clinical psychologists in India | **3,890** | Rehabilitation Council of India |
| → Children per qualified assessor | **≈ 5,000** | 1.98 cr ÷ 3,890 |
| Poor readers in Grade 3 still poor in Grade 9 | **74%** | Francis, Shaywitz, Stuebing, Shaywitz & Fletcher, 1996 |

Read those together and the problem states itself: **the queue is the diagnosis problem.**
It is not that people disagree about whether screening matters. It is that there are about
five thousand children for every person allowed to sign the assessment.

## The law already asks for this

The **Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016** recognises Specific Learning
Disability as a disability, and mandates:

- screening of **every school student** for SLD on completion of **age 8**
- resource rooms for remedial education in all schools
- examination provisions for affected students

So universal screening is not a proposal. It is an existing legal requirement.

## And there is already a tool — but it is a checklist

**NCERT PRASHAST** (Pre-Assessment Holistic Screening Tool) is the national instrument,
covering all 21 RPwD conditions.

- **Part 1** is filled in by the **regular class teacher**, by hand, from observation
- **Part 2** is validated by a special educator
- the child is then referred onward to an assessment camp

PRASHAST is a good piece of policy. But it is a subjective observation written on paper by
someone with thirty other children in the room, and it feeds into a queue that is 5,000
children deep.

## What is actually missing

Not the mandate. Not the intent. **The measurement.**

There is no step between "a teacher noticed something" and "a clinical psychologist has an
opening" that produces an objective, repeatable number.

That gap is the entire reason MENTIS exists.

## Why it has to be early

The window matters more than almost anything else. A child caught at 6 can be redirected.
A child caught at 11 is managing a gap that has already compounded for five years —
and by then the damage is rarely only academic:

- **The child is called lazy, not dyslexic.** The difficulty is invisible, so it gets
  blamed on effort.
- **Confidence breaks before the skill does.** By the time anyone tests, the child has
  often already stopped trying.

The 74% figure above is the quantitative version of that sentence.
